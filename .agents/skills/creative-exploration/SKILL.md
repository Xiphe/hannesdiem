# Creative Exploration

Keep the user in the making, not merely judging finished proposals. This skill governs collaboration; it supplies no aesthetic direction. Existing project instructions and supplied wording remain authoritative.

## Establish the question

Restore the project's decision record before proposing work. Name the current creative question and what the next comparison will help reveal. Keep it manageable without isolating variables that need each other to make sense. Follow the user's actual question rather than imposing a typography/layout/motion checklist.

Ground each direction in the user's words, references or accepted work: **observable mechanism → hypothesis against expressed intent**. Distinguish that hypothesis from an artistic meaning the user has supplied. When evidence cannot support meaningful options, interview for the missing direction; ask open questions that help the user express it rather than offering a biased menu. Never retrofit symbolism to arbitrary choices.

## Shared modes

Modes belong to a **question and scope**, not automatically to the whole project. Use these keywords when starting a comparison, changing phase, or checking alignment; avoid a status ceremony on every turn.

### Expansion — open the field

Default for unresolved creative direction. Offer either:

- Meaningfully different directions within the question, with enough tangible evidence to compare their mechanisms; or
- A manipulable experiment exposing consequential creative choices, with immediate feedback and recoverable states. A baseline toggle on one proposed concept is insufficient when the underlying direction is still open.

Choose the smallest artifact that makes the differences perceivable. Cosmetic variants of one premise do not provide breadth. Preserve accepted anchors while expanding relevant alternatives around them. No fixed option quota.

A **No** keeps the affected question open: use the rejection to explore other mechanisms, not just polish the rejected answer. If the mismatch is unclear, interview. “Better, but not it” and requests for more options remain Expansion. End a round with the differences or controls available for the user's response; do not silently synthesize a winner.

### Festhalten — keep a scoped Yes

Record an explicit Yes with its exact element, scope, evidence and artifact. “Use font A for section one” settles that typeface there; setting, motion and the rest of the scene remain open. “Go ahead and prototype” authorizes an experiment, not acceptance of its result. Tentative praise is feedback rather than a commitment.

Festhalten preserves an anchor inside either phase; it does not itself trigger narrowing elsewhere. Keep rejected and unjudged work distinguishable.

### Wendepunkt — agree to narrow

Proactively surface when there may be enough material to combine or choose. Name the affected scope, accepted anchors and remaining uncertainty. The user decides whether to turn; idea count, fatigue and isolated positive reactions are insufficient.

An explicit natural-language request to choose, combine or develop a direction already authorizes the turn within that scope. Recognize it; do not demand these exact keywords or ask for the same authorization again. If readiness is unclear, keep Expansion and clarify the proposed transition.

### Zusammenführung — combine and compress

Work toward a coherent answer within the agreed scope, using the chosen material and explicit tradeoffs. Evaluate in context; preserve accepted anchors. Supply focused comparisons or controls for unresolved details rather than continuously adding unrelated directions.

A rejected detail calls for a scoped adjustment. If uncertainty undermines the chosen direction, explicitly reopen that question in Expansion while retaining unaffected decisions. Keep the transition visible; neither phase changes silently.

## Continuity and execution

Maintain one compact decision record in the project's existing design documentation. Update it after meaningful feedback and before handoffs:

```text
Question / scope:
Mode:
Intent / evidence / constraints:
Accepted anchors: element, scope, user evidence, artifact
Rejected directions + reasons; parked/unjudged work:
Open alternatives / questions:
Next move:
```

Recover this state on fresh runs; do not restart settled questions or promote an unjudged experiment to an accepted direction. Explain new evidence before reopening an anchor.

The agent handles research, preparation and technical execution proactively inside the authorized question. Reserve questions for missing expressive direction and real decisions. An already-decided implementation request needs execution, not a fresh exploration ritual. Prototype creation, artistic acceptance and publication are separate scopes; preserve the authorization actually given.
