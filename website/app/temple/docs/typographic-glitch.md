# Temple title: typographic glitch references

Research: 2026-10-22. Exploration evidence; no artistic direction approved.

## Artist’s current constraints

- Four intact wordmarks: Germania One, Cinzel Decorative **Bold**, Unbounded, Bricolage Grotesque (D). Existing fonts; preserve each face’s natural spacing and kerning.
- Scroll moves the whole wordmark through these faces. Mouse directly disrupts individual characters; most of the title remains coherent. Local misalignment during disruption is acceptable.
- Preserve TEMPLE.right = OF.right; OF.left = SOUND.left. Reject fixed monospaced slots, random-font ransom-note composition and mere letter substitution presented as glitch.
- Interesting without an overly technical aesthetic. Continue expanding until the artist explicitly says “YES, that’s it”.

## References

### 1. M Firdaus — physical scanner distortion

[Creator’s image series](https://www.behance.net/gallery/25105905/Typography-studies-scanner-glitch). The creator identifies Motor Oil as the typeface and a Canon Pixma scanner as the production method. This is **static artwork**, not evidence for an animation or browser technique. Its relevance is actual physical corruption of a coherent typographic image, independent of RGB separation, terminal characters or digital-interface decoration. The source does not specify the scanning gestures or a reproducible process; do not invent them.

### 2. Tatiana Lapina / Creative Veila — ink and halftone offsets

[Creator’s six-effect gallery](https://www.veila.me/shop/typography-glitch-text-effects). The author describes a printer-inspired combination of halftone, paper texture and offsets; monochrome/inverted examples are visible alongside colored versions. These are Photoshop effects, **not interactive web effects**. Their useful distinction is material damage within recognizably typeset words. Our interpretation: borrowing displacement/overprint characteristics could avoid a cyberpunk default; neither paper texture nor halftone has been requested for Temple.

### 3. Lucas Bebber — trembling contours

[Runnable “Squiggly Text” and source](https://codepen.io/lbebber/pen/KwGEQv/). The text remains in one face while its contours change through five SVG turbulence/displacement filters. The source loops those states every 0.34 seconds. It demonstrates rough, uneven edges without fragmenting spelling or replacing glyphs. This is continuous whole-text agitation, **not** our intended localized, resolving interaction. A short, pointer-triggered application is an adaptation worth comparing, not a source-proven solution.

### 4. Manoela Ilic — clipped competing layers

[Article](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2017/12/21/css-glitch-effect/), [live demos](https://tympanus.net/Tutorials/CSSGlitchEffect/), [author’s slow-motion breakdown](https://tympanus.net/codrops/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/glitchvisualization2.gif). An intact base sits under translated copies exposed in changing slices. Authored keyframes include empty slices, producing pauses within the disturbance. The source uses color/blend treatments; neutral fragments from **different font layers** would be our adaptation. This supplies a genuine compositing mechanism beyond swapping complete letters, without requiring altered fonts.

### 5. Jorge Toloza — gesture-linked distortion

[Article and source](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2024/11/06/how-to-create-an-organic-text-distortion-effect-with-infinite-scrolling/), [live demo](https://tympanus.net/Tutorials/OrganicTextDistortion/). Lines move with sine/cosine offsets responsive to scrolling speed and direction. It demonstrates a perceivable relationship between gesture and deformation. It is fluid line motion, **not font glitch**, and should not become an excuse to replace the requested effect with wobbling text.

## Next shared exploration

Compare mechanisms before building another composite: clipped/displaced fragments versus rough contour disruption, in neutral color and with the same intact wordmark underneath. Separately compare how scroll exposes the next complete face: narrow migrating fragments versus broader interruptions. Mouse damage and scroll transitions need not use identical timing. These are candidate comparisons, not choices made for the artist.

No remote media downloaded; links point to creator-hosted galleries/demos. Static print references establish texture vocabulary, not animation timing. None of these sources establishes Temple’s emotional meaning or the final effect’s quality.
