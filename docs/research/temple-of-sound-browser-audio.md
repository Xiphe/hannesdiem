# Temple of Sound: browser audio constraints

2026-10-21. Investigation for [Establish the browser audio constraints](https://github.com/Xiphe/hannesdiem/issues/4). Facts and options; no artistic or implementation decision. Documentation review only, without device measurements.

## Timing and transport

- **Recorded voice:** `<audio>` provides play/pause, playback rate, a seconds-based `currentTime`, seekable ranges, and seeking/buffering events. Assigning `currentTime` seeks; loading and seeking are asynchronous. Native controls are available. Timed metadata cues can mark phrases; HTML recommends `cuechange` over rate-limited `timeupdate` for cue boundaries. Neither event promises frame-perfect animation. [HTML media](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/media.html#media-elements), [metadata cues](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/media.html#best-practices-for-metadata-text-tracks)
- **Shared audio clock:** Web Audio schedules sources and parameter automation against `AudioContext.currentTime`. Oscillators, buffers, gain and filters can generate a soundbed. `getOutputTimestamp()` estimates the relationship between output audio time and `performance.now()`; hardware/output latency still matters. [Web Audio 1.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#dom-audiocontext-getoutputtimestamp)
- **Two viable mechanisms:** a media element can feed a Web Audio graph while retaining its own transport; alternatively, decoded `AudioBuffer` sources share the context clock directly. A buffer source starts once: replay/seek requires another source, reusing the buffer and starting at an offset. `suspend()` freezes the whole context clock, including the bed. A cross-origin media element routed through Web Audio outputs silence without appropriate CORS access. [Media source](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#MediaElementAudioSourceNode), [buffer source](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#AudioBufferSourceNode), [suspension](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#dom-audiocontext-suspend)
- **Inference for prototypes:** derive each text/visual frame from the chosen transport position, rather than counting animation frames. Recompute after seeks, buffering and resume. Choose a clock only after deciding whether speech or visitor movement controls progression; routing media into a graph does not give it buffer-source scheduling semantics.

## Starting, interruption and mobile

- Audible autoplay can be blocked. Chrome documents suspended contexts requiring user interaction and `resume()`; media `play()` can reject. WebKit documents direct activation handlers for audible media. A deliberate sound-start action must handle success/failure; silent ambient visuals need not wait for audio. [Chrome policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay/), [WebKit policy](https://webkit.org/blog/6784/new-video-policies-for-ios/) (historical vendor explanations; current device behavior needs verification)
- The Web Audio 1.1 **working draft** specifies an `interrupted` state and recovery when the interruption ends, for example when another app takes the output device. This is not a universal browser-behavior guarantee. [Interruption model](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.1/#handling-an-interruption-on-the-audiocontext)
- Background pages may freeze or be discarded; mobile termination may have no final unload event. Save recoverable position on visibility change. Returning to the page must reconcile actual transport/context state with visitor intent. [Chrome lifecycle](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/page-lifecycle-api)
- A still-open WebKit report describes silent Web Audio after backgrounding an iOS Home Screen app, even when state reports running or resume is attempted. This establishes a reported failure mode, not prevalence or proof that current Safari tabs fail. [WebKit report](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=291892)
- Media Session can expose playback/seek handlers to platform media controls; it does not grant background execution. Treat it as an optional, feature-detected integration. [Media Session working draft](https://www.w3.org/TR/mediasession/)

## Access and unanswered decisions

- WCAG 1.4.2 requires independent stop/pause or volume control for automatically playing audio lasting over three seconds. Auto-starting visual movement lasting over five seconds alongside other content generally needs pause/stop/hide under 2.2.2, subject to its essential-activity exception. [Audio control](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-control.html), [motion control](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide)
- Nonessential interaction-triggered motion can be disabled under AAA 2.3.3. A reduced-motion presentation can retain text and synchronization without movement. Prerecorded audio-only content needs an equivalent alternative, except clearly identified audio alternatives for existing text; the final synchronized composition determines applicable media requirements. [Interaction motion](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html), [audio alternatives](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-only-and-video-only-prerecorded)
- **Artist decisions:** who leads timing; what happens when scrolling ahead/back; phrase versus word synchronization; whether voice and bed share pause/mute/seek; whether leaving a verse or backgrounding pauses the experience; what remains in silent/reduced-motion use. Browser capabilities do not settle these.
- **Evidence still needed after those decisions:** real iPhone Safari and Android tests of start, seeking, lock/background/return, output-device changes and recovery; measured audiovisual alignment under the chosen GPU load and output routes. No latency or uninterrupted-playback promise is established here.
